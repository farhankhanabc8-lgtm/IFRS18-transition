# IFRS 18 Transition: From IAS 1 to IFRS 18

Nov 26, 2026 · Farhan

IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements* replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Because it applies retrospectively, our 2026 figures become the restated comparative year, so the work starts now.

## Purpose and audience

This presentation explains what changes when we move from IAS 1 to IFRS 18, when it takes effect, and what the transition means for us. Each section below is one slide or a short group of slides.

- **Audience (assumed):** finance leadership, the audit committee and the reporting team. Please confirm.
- **Company (assumed):** a non-financial group with a 31 December year-end that reports under full IFRS. A different year-end shifts every date below.
- **Length (assumed):** 30 to 40 minutes, about 15 slides.

## From IAS 1 to IFRS 18: what changes and what stays

IFRS 18 changes how performance is presented, not how it is measured. Net profit, equity and cash do not move. Investors get a more comparable income statement.

| Area | IAS 1 today | IFRS 18 from 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income statement structure | Few required lines, no required operating profit | Five categories and two new required subtotals |
| Company KPIs such as adjusted EBITDA | Outside the financial statements, unaudited | Disclosed and reconciled in a note as MPMs, within audit scope |
| Grouping of line items | General materiality guidance | Explicit aggregation and disaggregation principles |
| Operating expenses | By nature or by function | By nature, function or mixed, with specified natures disclosed if by function |
| Recognition and measurement | Unchanged | Unchanged |

Much of IAS 1 (going concern, accruals, the balance sheet, equity statement and OCI) carries over to IFRS 18 or moves to IAS 8 with little change.

## Key dates

IFRS 18 is mandatory for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, and early adoption is permitted. For a 31 December year-end, the 2026 financial statements are the last under IAS 1, and the first IFRS 18 annual report covers 2027 and is published in early 2028.

## New income statement categories

Every item of income and expense goes into one of five categories. Operating is the default, so anything not classified elsewhere lands there.

| Category | What goes in it (non-financial company) |
| --- | --- |
| Operating | Revenue, cost of sales, selling and admin costs, impairments, FX on operating items, and anything not classified elsewhere |
| Investing | Income and expenses from associates and joint ventures (equity method), and from cash, cash equivalents and investments held separately from operations |
| Financing | Interest on borrowings and other liabilities that only raise finance, and interest on lease liabilities and pension liabilities |
| Income taxes | Tax expense under IAS 12 |
| Discontinued operations | As under IFRS 5 |

**Main business activities matter.** A company that invests in assets or provides financing to customers as a main business activity (such as a bank, insurer or investment entity) puts some of that income and expense in operating. We assume this does not apply to us. Please confirm.

## Required subtotals

IFRS 18 requires three totals and subtotals on the face of the income statement.

1. **Operating profit or loss:** all income and expenses in the operating category.
2. **Profit or loss before financing and income taxes:** operating plus investing. A company whose main activity is financing customers may be exempt from this one.
3. **Profit or loss:** as today.

Operating profit is defined by the standard, so it may differ from the EBIT we report today. A common example is share of profit from associates, which moves out of operating into investing.

## Management-defined performance measures (MPMs)

Any subtotal of income and expenses that we use in public communications outside the financial statements to show management's view of performance becomes an MPM. It must be disclosed in a single note, and the auditors will cover it.

**What counts:** for example, adjusted operating profit or underlying profit, if used in results announcements, investor presentations or the annual report narrative. Subtotals that IFRS already lists (such as gross profit or operating profit before depreciation and amortisation) are not MPMs.

**What we must disclose for each MPM:**

- why it is useful and how it is calculated
- a reconciliation to the most directly comparable IFRS subtotal
- the income tax and non-controlling interest effect of each reconciling item
- any change in how it is calculated, and why

**Slide to build:** an inventory of the measures we publish today, and whether each one stays, changes or is dropped. We assume we report some adjusted measures. Please confirm which.

## Aggregation and disaggregation

Items are grouped when they share characteristics and split when they do not, if the information is material. The primary statements give a structured summary, and the notes give the detail.

- **Labels:** "Other" is allowed only when no more informative label fits, and we must explain what it contains.
- **Operating expenses:** present them by nature, by function, or mixed, whichever gives the most useful summary.
- **If by function:** disclose five natures in a single note: depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses, and inventory write-downs.
- **Balance sheet:** new required line items include goodwill, shown separately from other intangibles.

**Slide to build:** where our "other income" and "other expenses" lines need splitting. This needs our 2026 note detail.

## Knock-on changes to other standards

| Standard | Change from 2027 |
| --- | --- |
| IAS 7 Statement of Cash Flows | The indirect method starts from operating profit. For most non-financial companies, interest and dividends paid go to financing, and interest and dividends received go to investing, so today's choices end. |
| IAS 33 Earnings per Share | Extra per-share amounts are allowed only if based on an IFRS total or subtotal, or an MPM. |
| IAS 34 Interim Reporting | Interim reports in 2027 use the new categories and subtotals, and include MPM disclosures. |
| IAS 8 | Takes over the IAS 1 basics that IFRS 18 does not carry (basis of preparation, compliance statement). |

## How the transition from IAS 1 works

IFRS 18 applies retrospectively under IAS 8. The 2027 financial statements show 2026 restated as if IFRS 18 had always applied.

1. **Restate comparatives:** re-present the 2026 income statement, cash flow statement and notes under the new categories and subtotals.
2. **Reconcile in the first year:** for the 2026 comparative, disclose a line-by-line reconciliation from the IAS 1 income statement to the IFRS 18 presentation.
3. **Interims follow:** the first 2027 interim report uses the new headings and subtotals, with the same kind of reconciliation.
4. **No number changes:** profit, equity and cash are unchanged, so there is no opening balance adjustment.
5. **Early adoption:** allowed, but we must disclose it. With five weeks left, we assume we adopt on the mandatory date.
